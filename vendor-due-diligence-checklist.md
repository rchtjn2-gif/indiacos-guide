# Vendor due diligence for Indian SMEs: a 5-step checklist

Most supplier fraud against small businesses is not clever. It relies on nobody checking. These five steps use public records and take about fifteen minutes per vendor, which is cheap compared with chasing an advance that never turns into goods.

## 1. Confirm the entity exists and is active

If the vendor says it is a private limited company or an LLP, it has a CIN or LLPIN registered with the Ministry of Corporate Affairs. Check that the status is "Active", not "Strike Off", "Under Process of Striking Off" or "Dormant". A proprietorship has no MCA record, so lean harder on the GST and bank checks below.

## 2. Match GSTIN, PAN and legal name

Characters 3 to 12 of a GSTIN are the business's PAN, and the first two digits are the state code. The legal name on the GST registration should match the name on the invoice and on the bank account. A brand name on the letterhead is fine, but the legal name is who you can actually pursue. The [GSTIN format guide](https://indiacos.com/gstin-format) explains every character, and the [GSTIN search](https://indiacos.com/gstin-search) checks the check digit and shows the registration status.

## 3. Look for court cases naming the business

A single old case means little. A pattern of recovery suits, cheque-bounce complaints under Section 138 of the Negotiable Instruments Act, or insolvency petitions is a warning. Remember that a name match is not proof of identity, so confirm the parties before drawing conclusions.

## 4. Check the directors or partners

Look at who runs the company and what else they direct. Many companies struck off under one director, or a director who joined last month, are worth a question. IndiaCos lists [directors A to Z](https://indiacos.com/directors) with the companies they are linked to.

## 5. Verify the bank account independently

Call the vendor on a number you found yourself, not one printed on the invoice, before the first payment and whenever bank details "change". Most invoice-redirection fraud is caught here.

## A one-page record

Keep a short file per vendor: CIN or LLPIN and status, GSTIN and status, PAN, legal name, directors, court-case notes, the date you checked, and who checked. Re-check yearly, or before any large order.

For volume, the [IndiaCos API](https://indiacos.com/api) returns company and GST data as JSON so the checklist can run inside your onboarding form. For anything high-stakes, confirm against the issuing authority's own record.
