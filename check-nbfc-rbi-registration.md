# Is this lender RBI-registered? Checking an NBFC

Loan apps, gold loan companies, P2P lending platforms and "investment" schemes often describe themselves as finance companies. In India, a non-banking financial company (NBFC) generally needs a **Certificate of Registration from the Reserve Bank of India** to carry on that business. Checking registration takes a minute and should come before you share documents, borrow or invest.

## What the register shows

IndiaCos lists RBI-registered NBFCs and asset reconstruction companies, A to Z, with each entry linked to the company's record. Its [NBFC register page](https://indiacos.com/nbfc) shows **8,529 registered entities** (October 2026). For each one you can see:

- the **company name**, including former names,
- the **NBFC type**,
- the **regulatory layer**,
- the **CIN** (Corporate Identification Number),
- the city.

## Types you will come across

RBI classifies NBFCs by activity. Common types in the register include Investment and Credit Company, NBFC-Factor, NBFC-Microfinance Institution, Housing Finance Company, Infrastructure Finance Company, Core Investment Company, Account Aggregator, Asset Reconstruction Company and **Peer-to-Peer Lending Platform**. IndiaCos lists **28 RBI-registered P2P lending platforms**, so if a P2P app is not among them, that is a serious warning sign.

## Layers

Under RBI's scale-based regulation, NBFCs are placed in layers (base, middle, upper and top) depending on size and risk, with stricter rules as you go up. The register shows each company's layer, which tells you how closely it is supervised.

## A five-minute checklist

1. **Get the legal name.** Apps often use a brand name. Ask for, or look in the app's terms for, the registered company name and CIN.
2. **Find it on the register.** Search by name or browse by type. Former names are shown, which helps with renamed companies.
3. **Match the CIN.** The CIN on the register should match the one in the loan agreement or sanction letter.
4. **Check the type fits the activity.** A company registered as a core investment company should not be offering you a personal loan.
5. **Look at the company record.** IndiaCos links each NBFC to its company page with incorporation details and directors, so you can see who runs it.

## If the lender is a bank's partner

Many digital lending apps are not lenders themselves; they are partners of a bank or NBFC that actually lends. The loan agreement should name the regulated lender. Check that entity, not just the app.

## Other registers worth knowing

Regulated finance is not only NBFCs. IndiaCos also lists SEBI-registered intermediaries such as brokers and AIFs, and insolvency announcements, from its [regulatory hub](https://indiacos.com/regulatory). A company that appears in insolvency announcements is not one to lend money to.

## Bottom line

No registration, a mismatched CIN or a type that does not fit the activity are reasons to stop. Registration does not make an investment safe, but its absence is a clear red flag.
